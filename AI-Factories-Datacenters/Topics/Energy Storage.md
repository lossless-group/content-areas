# Snapshot

_Energy Storage is the infrastructure layer that captures electricity or other forms of energy, holds it, and releases it when supply, demand, or grid conditions make that energy more valuable. The category is expanding from lithium-ion batteries into long-duration, thermal, mechanical, hydrogen, software, and hybrid power-plant architectures._

> “Global battery storage installations jumped by 43% in 2025.” [^r63byo]

This profile captures the category at a moment when storage is moving from a supporting asset for renewable generation to a core reliability, capacity, and firm-power market. Market estimates vary substantially because some reports measure equipment revenue, others installed capacity, and others the broader energy-storage-systems economy. [^vi2gyx] [^2z4lkq] [^t77wca] [^f0wobd] [^a9ivyh]

# What is this Market Category?

Energy Storage includes technologies, systems, software, and project services that shift energy across time: charging when electricity is abundant or inexpensive and discharging when it is scarce, expensive, or operationally necessary. Products are sold to utilities, independent power producers, renewable developers, commercial and industrial customers, data centers, microgrids, vehicle fleets, and households.

The category includes short-duration battery energy-storage systems, utility-scale battery plants, residential and commercial storage, long-duration energy storage, pumped hydro, thermal storage, compressed-air systems, gravity storage, hydrogen-based storage, battery-management systems, energy-management software, and integration or operation services. Battery energy-storage systems are currently the commercial center of gravity, while long-duration technologies compete to serve multi-hour or multi-day reliability needs. [^5dptk9] [^r63byo] [^eb0x7g]

Energy Storage excludes electricity generation by itself, transmission and distribution infrastructure without a storage function, ordinary fuel inventories, and electric vehicles unless the vehicle or charging system is explicitly being used as a grid-storage asset. Battery manufacturing is adjacent rather than identical to storage deployment: cell production, cathode materials, recycling, and storage-project integration participate in the value chain but should not automatically be counted as the same market.

The boundary is disputed at the edges: some market reports include hydrogen, thermal storage, pumped hydro, and storage software in one broad category, while narrower analysts restrict the category to electrochemical and grid-connected storage systems. [^2z4lkq] [^f0wobd] [^a9ivyh]

# Why Now?

- **Renewables created a time-shifting requirement.** Solar and wind growth produces increasingly frequent periods when generation is abundant but demand is low, followed by periods when generation falls or demand rises; storage converts that intermittency into dispatchable capacity. Reuters reported that surging solar installations and U.S. federal investment tax credits helped drive a 53% increase in U.S. battery installations in 2025. [^r63byo]

- **The cost curve has crossed a commercial threshold.** Global average battery costs have fallen by 90% since 2010, according to the International Energy Agency as reported by Reuters, reducing the cost of storing electricity and enabling larger projects. [^r63byo]

- **Policy has made storage financeable as a standalone asset.** U.S. investment tax credits for storage prompted a major installation increase, while developers accelerated projects before stricter tax-credit rules expected to take effect in 2026. [^9vnqbg] [^r63byo]

- **Reliability has become a procurement priority.** Utilities and large electricity consumers increasingly need firm power for extreme weather, grid outages, electrification, and data-center demand. California’s long-duration program describes Form Energy’s iron-air system as supporting up to 100 hours of continuous power during outages and extreme-weather events. [^eb0x7g]

- **Energy security has elevated storage from climate infrastructure to strategic infrastructure.** The International Energy Agency said energy innovation has entered a “security-focused phase” and that storage has moved to the forefront of global innovation activity as the world enters the “Age of Electricity.” [^j0vs3v]

- **Capital has broadened the technology set.** Funding is no longer concentrated only in lithium-ion deployment: Form Energy has raised more than $2 billion in equity and secured a $270 million credit facility, while energy-storage platforms are also attracting infrastructure, strategic, and climate investors. [^1uv8a5] [^k5evhz]

# What’s Happening?

## CAGR and TAM

- **Broad energy-storage market:** The Business Research Company estimates that the global energy-storage market will reach **$219.63 billion by 2030**, growing at a **15.4% CAGR**; its framing is a broad market forecast rather than a narrow installed-capacity measure. [^2z4lkq]

- **Battery energy-storage systems:** A ResearchAndMarkets listing estimates the global battery-energy-storage-system market at **$50.81 billion in 2025**, rising to **$105.96 billion by 2030** at a **15.8% CAGR**. [^t77wca]

- **Grid-scale battery storage:** Grand View Research estimates the global grid-scale battery-storage market at **$13.3 billion in 2025**, rising to **$42.4 billion by 2033** at a **14.2% CAGR**. [^vi2gyx] Another market estimate projects **$37.55 billion by 2030** at a much higher **30.8% CAGR**, illustrating how definitions, inclusion criteria, and forecast windows materially change the apparent market velocity. [^9pungp]

- **Installed-capacity view:** Mordor Intelligence forecasts energy-storage capacity to grow from **0.54 terawatt in 2026 to 1.52 terawatt in 2031**, a **23.05% CAGR**; this capacity-based estimate should not be compared directly with revenue-based forecasts. [^f0wobd]

- **Broad systems view:** A ResearchAndMarkets summary estimates the global energy-storage-systems market at **$266.84 billion in 2025**, growing to **$385.93 billion by 2030** at a **7.7% CAGR**, with battery storage the fastest-growing technology segment at **11.3%** over 2025–2030. [^a9ivyh]

## Category creation events

- **Grid storage became a standalone infrastructure category rather than an extension of generation.** The combination of standalone U.S. storage tax credits, renewable overbuild, and reliability procurement made storage financeable as an independent asset class; Reuters reported that developers accelerated projects ahead of expected 2026 tax-credit changes. [^9vnqbg]

- **Long-duration storage acquired a defining reference architecture.** Form Energy’s iron-air technology is designed to discharge continuously for approximately 100 hours, positioning multi-day storage as a distinct product category rather than simply a longer-duration lithium-ion battery. [^1uv8a5] [^eb0x7g]

- **The category has expanded from batteries to a portfolio of storage technologies.** Energy Vault markets gravity-based, battery-based, and green-hydrogen-based systems under an energy-management and integration platform, reflecting the market’s movement toward technology-agnostic storage portfolios. [^5dptk9]

## Capital concentration

- **Form Energy is a leading concentration point for long-duration-storage capital.** The company raised a **$750 million Series G in August 2026** at an approximately **$2.5 billion post-money valuation**, bringing completed private financing above **$2.27 billion** when debt is included. [^1uv8a5]

- **Asset-heavy deployment platforms are attracting billion-dollar pools of capital.** A 2026 startup-fundraising survey identified Intersect Power, Zenobē, Form Energy, Fluence, Hithium, and Origis Energy as platforms that had each reached at least **$1 billion** in funding or capital formation. [^k5evhz]

- **Public and strategic capital is following the deployment curve.** Fluence has raised more than $1 billion according to a 2026 startup-fundraising compilation, while Energy Vault’s financing history includes strategic and infrastructure investors such as SoftBank and Orion Infrastructure Capital. [^k5evhz]

- **Geographic capital concentration remains uneven.** China dominates battery manufacturing, with annual manufacturing capacity forecast to reach **7,500 GWh by 2035**, while U.S. capacity is projected to rise to approximately **1,000 GWh**; the U.S. produced around **12 GWh** of battery-storage capacity in 2025 and is expected to reach roughly **30 GWh by 2030**. [^r63byo]

# Frontier and Open Questions

- **Will long-duration storage become a separate asset class or remain a feature inside a broader battery-storage market?** Form Energy is the clearest Challenger testing the multi-day-storage thesis, while Tesla, Fluence, and Energy Vault are more likely to determine whether customers buy duration as a technology-specific product or as a software-optimized service. [^5dptk9] [^1uv8a5] [^eb0x7g]

- **Can iron-air, flow, zinc-air, and thermal systems achieve bankability at utility scale?** Form Energy, e-Zinc, Antora, Rondo, and other Innovators must prove manufacturing yield, degradation, warrantyability, and project-finance performance against lithium-ion incumbents. [^1uv8a5] [^eb0x7g]

- **Will storage revenues come primarily from energy arbitrage, capacity, ancillary services, or reliability contracts?** Fluence, Tesla, utilities, and independent power producers are positioned to resolve the question through operating portfolios and market-design participation. [^5dptk9] [^r63byo]

- **Does the category include industrial heat storage?** Rondo and Antora treat stored heat as energy storage, while narrower battery-market definitions exclude it; the answer will determine whether the category is measured in electricity revenue, delivered thermal energy, or both.

- **Will policy uncertainty slow deployment or merely shift project timing?** Reuters reported a possible reduction of up to 16.5 GW in U.S. storage deployment from 2025 to 2029 under policy uncertainty, while the underlying demand for renewable integration and firm power continues to support long-term growth. [^9vnqbg]

- **Will China’s manufacturing scale make storage a hardware commodity?** China is forecast to reach 7,500 GWh of annual battery-manufacturing capacity by 2035, while U.S. capacity is expected to reach about 1,000 GWh; CATL, BYD, Hithium, Tesla, and domestic-policy-led challengers will shape the resulting price and supply-chain dynamics. [^r63byo]

- **Will storage expand into a distributed flexibility market?** Tesla, Octopus Energy, Zenobē, and software-focused operators are positioned to connect residential batteries, vehicle fleets, commercial loads, and grid assets into coordinated virtual power plants rather than selling only standalone storage projects.

# Adjacent Concepts and Categories

- **Long-Duration Energy Storage** — The multi-hour and multi-day segment seeking to solve reliability gaps that short-duration lithium-ion systems do not fully cover.
- **Grid Flexibility** — The broader operating category connecting storage, demand response, flexible generation, and transmission optimization.
- **Virtual Power Plants** — Software-coordinated networks of batteries, vehicles, buildings, and flexible loads that act as a dispatchable grid resource.
- **Renewable Energy Integration** — The generation-and-grid problem that creates demand for storage by shifting variable solar and wind output.
- **Battery Management Systems** — Hardware and software that monitor cells, manage safety, estimate state of charge, and extend battery life.
- **Energy Arbitrage** — The commercial strategy of charging during low-price periods and discharging during high-price periods.
- **Firm Power** — Dispatchable electricity available when renewable generation is insufficient or grid conditions are stressed.
- **Industrial Thermal Storage** — Storage of heat rather than electricity for industrial processes, a major boundary-expansion thesis represented by thermal-storage innovators.
