# Snapshot

_**Liquid Cooling for Data Centers** is the infrastructure category that replaces or supplements air cooling with liquid-based heat removal for increasingly dense AI, high-performance-computing, and cloud workloads. Its center of gravity is shifting from specialist deployments to a mainstream data-center design requirement as rack densities exceed what conventional air systems can economically and physically support._

> “The data center liquid cooling market size was valued at USD 5.52 billion in 2025 and is estimated to reach USD 18.79 billion by 2031, at a CAGR of 22.65%.” [^pgr27w]

The profile captures the category as of **October 2026**, when AI infrastructure demand, GPU thermal density, water and power constraints, and hyperscaler deployment plans are converging. The category is worth tracking because it is no longer limited to cooling hardware: it increasingly includes cold plates, coolant-distribution units, immersion tanks, fluids, heat exchangers, monitoring software, services, and facility-level integration.

# What is this Market Category?

Liquid Cooling for Data Centers comprises technologies that use a liquid to collect and transport heat from servers, processors, memory, or entire racks to a heat-rejection system. The principal approaches are **direct-to-chip cooling**, in which coolant flows through cold plates attached to processors; **single-phase [[Immersion Cooling]]**, in which servers are submerged in a dielectric liquid that remains liquid; and **two-phase immersion cooling**, in which the fluid boils and condenses inside a sealed tank. [[Direct-to-Chip Liquid Cooling]] is currently described as the leading technology by deployment volume, while immersion is growing rapidly as rack densities rise. [^jb9nrv]

The category sells primarily to hyperscalers, colocation providers, enterprise data-center operators, high-performance-computing laboratories, AI-cloud providers, server OEMs, and data-center engineering and construction firms. The product stack can include cold plates, manifolds, pumps, coolant-distribution units, tanks, dielectric fluids, facility heat exchangers, controls, commissioning, maintenance, and retrofit services. [^jb9nrv]

The category excludes ordinary computer-room air-conditioning, conventional air-cooled server racks, generic building HVAC without a data-center liquid interface, and industrial process cooling that is not designed for computing infrastructure. It also excludes most chip-level thermal-management products used only inside consumer devices unless they are adapted for data-center server or rack deployment.

The boundary is disputed at the facility edge: some market definitions include chillers, cooling towers, and broader thermal-management infrastructure, while others count only liquid-contact equipment; it is also contested whether coolant fluids, services, and hybrid air/liquid systems should be counted as part of the core category.

# Why Now?

- **AI rack density has crossed the practical limits of air cooling.** Market coverage identifies direct-to-chip liquid cooling as the leading deployed technology and immersion as the fastest-growing approach as extreme rack densities become more common. [^jb9nrv] The enabling shift is not simply greater compute demand: it is the concentration of heat in GPU and accelerator systems, which makes liquid heat transfer increasingly necessary at the rack level.

- **Liquid cooling is becoming an architectural requirement rather than an optional efficiency upgrade.** One market estimate projects the category from **$5.52 billion in 2025 to $18.79 billion in 2031**, implying a 22.65% CAGR. [^pgr27w] That trajectory reflects customers designing new AI facilities around liquid-ready power, piping, distribution, and heat rejection rather than attempting to bolt cooling onto air-first buildings.

- **The retrofit market is expanding because direct-to-chip systems can fit existing server architectures.** Market Research Future reports that direct-to-chip represented approximately 46% of category share in 2025 and attributes its position to integration with standard server form factors and comparatively limited facility redesign. [^urbgs2] This lowers adoption friction for enterprise and colocation operators that cannot immediately rebuild entire facilities.

- **Water, power, and sustainability constraints are making thermal efficiency a procurement issue.** Market coverage links growth to sustainability, AI and HPC workloads, and GPU-as-a-service adoption. [^pybb8x] The force is broader than regulation: operators increasingly evaluate cooling as part of power availability, operating cost, water use, and the usable compute density of a site.

- **Incumbent recognition is reducing perceived deployment risk.** Vertiv launched a liquid-cooling services offering in February 2025, while Carrier announced an investment and technology partnership with ZutaCore for waterless two-phase direct-to-chip cooling. [^0ds78k] [^f76psz] These moves give buyers access to established infrastructure and service channels, helping move liquid cooling from specialist procurement toward standard data-center sourcing.

# Frontier and Open Questions

- **Will direct-to-chip remain the default architecture, or will immersion capture the highest-density AI clusters?** MarketsandMarkets identifies direct-to-chip as the current deployment leader and immersion as the fastest-growing technology. [^jb9nrv] Incumbents such as Vertiv and Schneider are best positioned to make direct-to-chip the enterprise default, while LiquidStack and Submer are positioned to prove the immersion case.

- **Can waterless two-phase cooling scale beyond specialist deployments?** Carrier’s investment and partnership with ZutaCore gives waterless two-phase direct-to-chip cooling industrial validation, but the supplied sources do not establish broad production scale. [^f76psz] ZutaCore and Carrier will drive resolution through deployment economics, serviceability, fluid management, and compatibility with standard server platforms.

- **Will customers buy liquid cooling as a component, a rack, or a complete facility system?** The arrival of Vertiv Liquid Cooling Services indicates that lifecycle operations and availability are becoming part of the purchase decision. [^0ds78k] Incumbents such as Vertiv, Schneider, Eaton, and Carrier are likely to push the category toward integrated infrastructure contracts, while specialists will try to preserve component-level margins.

- **How much of the reported market size is genuinely liquid-contact equipment rather than adjacent thermal infrastructure?** Forecasts range from a 20.1% CAGR and $29.5 billion by 2033 to a 51.93% CAGR and $10.70 billion by 2030, showing substantial scope variation. [^5ypyim] [^pybb8x] Analysts and buyers need a common taxonomy separating cold plates, CDUs, tanks, fluids, chillers, services, and facility construction.

- **Will retrofit compatibility determine the winning architecture?** Direct-to-chip is reported to have captured approximately 46% of 2025 share partly because it can integrate with standard server form factors and require less facility redesign. [^urbgs2] Chilldyne, Iceotope, JetCool, and Vertiv are positioned to resolve the retrofit question, while immersion vendors must demonstrate lower total cost and manageable equipment reconfiguration.

- **Will liquid cooling become a standard feature of server and rack platforms rather than a separate market?** Supermicro is already included among the named AI data-center cooling participants, indicating that server OEMs are moving into the thermal stack. [^jb9nrv] Supermicro and other server manufacturers could compress specialist margins by bundling cooling into complete GPU and rack-scale systems.

# Adjacent Concepts and Categories

- **AI Data Centers** — The primary demand engine creating extreme rack densities and making liquid cooling economically necessary.
- **High-Performance Computing** — A long-standing workload category in which thermal density and uptime make advanced cooling valuable.
- **Data-Center Infrastructure** — The broader category containing power, racks, monitoring, thermal systems, and facility integration.
- **Direct-to-Chip Cooling** — The dominant liquid-cooling architecture in which cold plates remove heat directly from processors.
- **Immersion Cooling** — An alternative architecture that submerges servers in dielectric liquid, either in single-phase or two-phase systems.
- **Coolant Distribution Units** — The rack- or row-level equipment that circulates and conditions liquid between facility loops and server cold plates.
- **Dielectric Fluids** — Electrically nonconductive liquids used in immersion systems and some specialized liquid-cooling architectures.
- **Data-Center Power and Water Efficiency** — The operating and sustainability lens through which cooling performance is increasingly evaluated.
