_Thermal Management is the market for hardware, controls, and software that keep high‑density built environments—especially smart buildings and data centers—within safe temperature and humidity envelopes while minimizing energy use and downtime._  
_It clusters previously separate niches (HVAC control, building energy management, data center cooling) into a single performance‑critical stack where AI, automation, and advanced cooling methods are now a strategic differentiator._

# What is this Market Category?

Thermal Management is the facility‑scale market for technologies that monitor, control, and dissipate heat in built environments—primarily commercial buildings and data centers—so that equipment, occupants, and processes operate within safe temperature and humidity bands. [^fzmwc2] [^7r8s7t] [^3dti22]  
On the data‑center side, this includes cooling equipment (CRAC/CRAH units, in‑row and in‑rack coolers, liquid cooling loops, heat‑rejection systems) and control software that regulate temperature, airflow, and humidity for IT loads. [^fzmwc2] [^7r8s7t] [^3dti22] [^f66u0q]  
On the building side, it includes building energy management systems (BEMS) and energy‑management platforms that integrate sensors, HVAC controls, and analytics to optimize energy consumption and comfort across large portfolios. [^0ft83v] [^c7123f] [^kbn4js] [^6nysw7]  
Products are sold to facilities owners and operators (commercial real estate, campus operators, enterprise data‑center teams, colocation providers, hyperscale clouds) and, increasingly, to ESG‑driven corporate sustainability teams who own energy‑efficiency targets. [^0ft83v] [^kbn4js] [^3dti22]  

The category explicitly excludes chip‑level and device‑level thermal interface materials, automotive battery thermal systems, and general consumer HVAC appliances, because those are typically treated as separate component or vertical markets with different buyers and economics. [^fzmwc2] [^7r8s7t] [^3dti22]  
Boundary disputes show up around whether thermal management should encompass whole‑building energy‑management platforms (which also handle lighting and demand response) and whether hyperscaler‑internal cooling innovation—for example, custom liquid‑cooling deployments—is part of the same market or a separate, proprietary practice.

- [[content-areas/AI-Factories-Datacenters/Concepts/Smart Buildings|Smart‑building]] and digital‑twin adoption are creating the data substrate needed for AI‑driven thermal optimization, making energy‑management and data‑center cooling markets more receptive to software‑first solutions.   Multiple reports emphasize that EMS/BEMS solutions combine monitoring, analytics, and control to improve building energy performance, which is a natural fit for machine‑learning‑based optimization. [^0ft83v] [^c7123f] [^kbn4js]  
  Data‑center cooling studies similarly highlight integrated hardware and control systems that regulate temperature, humidity, and airflow, providing clear integration points for AI and automation. [^fzmwc2] [^3dti22]

- Capital markets and analyst firms have started to treat data‑center cooling and building energy management as distinct global segments, with overlapping but specific coverage, giving founders a clearer category story and LPs a more visible thesis. # Frontier and Open Questions

- How far will hyperscale cloud providers vertically integrate thermal management (designing their own liquid‑cooling and control systems) versus relying on incumbent vendors, and which incumbents or challengers will be allowed into those stacks?  
  Incumbents like Schneider Electric and Vertiv, and innovators in liquid‑cooling hardware, are central to this question.

- Will AI‑driven building‑optimization platforms (BrainBox AI, PassiveLogic, 75F) become the default control layer on top of incumbent BMS hardware, or will incumbents successfully absorb these capabilities into their own platforms?  
  Challengers and innovators are most likely to push for overlay models, while incumbents may favor integrated suites.

- Can novel physical approaches, such as radiative cooling (SkyCool Systems) or advanced membranes (Evercloak), achieve cost and reliability parity with traditional mechanical cooling in mission‑critical environments like data centers?  
  Innovators are driving experimentation, but incumbents will decide large‑scale adoption.

- Will the thermal‑management category expand to formally include industrial process cooling and semiconductor manufacturing facilities, or contract to focus on building and data‑center environments only?  
  Incumbents with cross‑vertical footprints have the strongest influence on how analysts and investors draw these boundaries.

- How will regional policy (e.g., building performance standards, data‑center energy caps) reshape demand for thermal‑management retrofits versus greenfield deployments over the next decade?  
  Incumbents and challengers with strong local presence, particularly in high‑growth markets like the US, will shape responses. [^w6b0kl] [^82gmcf]

- Will BEMS and EMS markets converge with broader “agentic workspaces” and facility‑operations platforms, embedding thermal management as one of many autonomous processes, or remain specialized domains managed by facilities engineers?  
  Innovators in autonomous building control and AI‑for‑operations are most likely to push toward convergence.
