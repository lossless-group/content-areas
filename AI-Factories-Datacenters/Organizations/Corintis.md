## Value Proposition & Features

Corintis develops **in-chip microfluidic cooling**: microscale coolant channels are embedded in semiconductor structures to remove heat closer to its source. The company says this can deliver **10× more heat removal** and **50% greater energy efficiency**, targeting the thermal limits of future AI and high-performance-computing systems. [^E1]

Its target applications are AI chips, GPUs, and CPUs deployed in data centers, where higher compute density increases cooling and electricity demands. [^qyh1qi]

Priority features:

- Embedded microscale cooling channels inside or near the chip. [^E1]
- Direct heat extraction from high-power semiconductor devices. [^E1]
- Claimed 10× improvement in heat removal. [^E1]
- Claimed 50% improvement in cooling energy efficiency. [^E1]
- Compatibility focus on AI chips, GPUs, and CPUs. [^qyh1qi]
- Designed for data-center deployment. [^qyh1qi]
- Intended to reduce cooling-related electricity consumption. [^qyh1qi]
- EPFL-linked deep-tech development base in Lausanne. [^E1]

# History and Origin Story

Corintis is a Lausanne-based EPFL spin-off developing semiconductor cooling through embedded microfluidic channels. The company’s public materials identify EPFL Innovation Park as its headquarters, while recent coverage names Remco van Erp, Sam Harrison, and EPFL professor Elison Matioli as founders. # Competitive Landscape

## Who it's for, who it's not for

Corintis is aimed at semiconductor designers, AI-infrastructure companies, data-center operators, and cloud providers that need to dissipate heat from increasingly dense GPUs, CPUs, and other high-power processors. [^qyh1qi]

It is not positioned as a general-purpose software cooling optimizer, consumer electronics product, or standard facility-level HVAC vendor; its differentiation is hardware integrated into the semiconductor cooling path. [^E1] This makes it complementary to broader [[concepts/Market-Categories/Datacenter Operations|Datacenter Operations]] tooling rather than a direct substitute for it.

## Viable Alternatives

- **Traditional air cooling:** Lower-complexity cooling for less thermally demanding systems, but less suitable for extreme chip heat densities. [^E1]
- **Conventional liquid cooling:** A more established alternative that removes heat near servers or packages rather than through channels embedded in the chip. [^0zb8sw]
- **3D-printed or additively manufactured cold plates:** Competing approaches optimize cooling pathways around chip hot spots without necessarily embedding channels in the silicon. [^b6a3q7]
- **Other microfluidic cooling suppliers:** The closest technical alternatives pursue coolant channels within chip packages or adjacent semiconductor structures. [^exm8u0]
