# Defining and Describing Patient Capital

- _Patient capital gives worthwhile ideas time to become durable businesses._
- Patient capital is financing supplied with a long time horizon, tolerance for risk and short-term underperformance, and little expectation of a quick profit. [^eaz3ej] [^l0svvm] [^xiq3f9] It can take the form of equity or debt; the defining feature is the investor’s behavior rather than the identity of the recipient. [^ni61lj] The approach is especially relevant to climate technology, biotechnology, infrastructure, public services, and impact-oriented businesses whose markets or technologies may take years to mature. [^l0svvm] [^d62169]

## Uses in Context

- In venture finance, “patient capital” describes funding that lets science- and engineering-intensive startups develop products before pursuing rapid revenue or an exit. [^l0svvm]
- In impact investing, it refers to philanthropic or concessionary money accepting below-market or delayed returns to support enterprises serving people living in poverty. [^d4qy5h] [^5un1d1]
- In infrastructure and climate finance, the term describes capital willing to wait through long construction, regulatory, and payback periods. [^l0svvm] [^fx9p1t]
- In development finance, it can describe long-term investment in enterprises, institutions, or entrepreneurs whose social and economic benefits compound over time. [^d62169] [^d4qy5h]
- In corporate-finance research, patient capital is associated with long-term holding, reduced liquidity pressure, and the ability to sustain investment through economic cycles. [^xiq3f9] [^lkl2fl]

## History of Use

### Origins

- The concept emerged from discussions of long-term financing and the “varieties of capitalism” framework, where financial systems differ in their relationships between investors and firms. [^eaz3ej]
- Jacqueline Novogratz helped establish the modern impact-investing usage through Acumen’s practice and a 2007 academic publication, framing patient capital as a way to finance enterprises addressing poverty while allowing time for scale and sustainability. [^22bic9]
- The underlying idea extends beyond venture funds: examples include parents financing education, state investment in innovative enterprises, and entrepreneurs investing in long-lived infrastructure. [^d62169]

### Evolution

- **2007 — Impact-investing formulation:** Acumen’s model made patient capital a practical instrument for backing high-impact enterprises with long horizons and flexible expectations about returns. [^22bic9] [^d4qy5h]
- **2010s — Expansion into transformative technology:** The concept was applied to capital-intensive fields such as climate technology, human health, advanced systems, and infrastructure, where conventional venture timelines can be too short. [^l0svvm]
- **2020s — Measurement and firm-level research:** Academic studies increasingly connected patient capital with innovation, technology breakthroughs, resilience, ESG performance, and sustained investment across economic cycles. [^xiq3f9] [^ni61lj] [^lkl2fl]

## Best Real-World Examples

- [Acumen](https://acumen.org/patient-capital/) — deploys philanthropic capital into high-impact enterprises serving people living in poverty. [^d4qy5h]
- [The Engine](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/working-definitions/what-is-patient-capital) — supports “tough tech” ventures in fields such as climate technology, health, and advanced infrastructure that may require years of development. [^l0svvm]
- [Nesta Impact Investments](https://investment-note.janpia.or.jp/n/nc3804d10155c?hl=en) — demonstrates a foundation-linked investment model with long holding periods and early-stage technology financing. [^22bic9]
- [Haqdarshak Empowerment Solutions](https://acumen.org/patient-capital/) — uses patient-backed enterprise development to connect underserved Indians with public benefits while creating dignified work. [^d4qy5h]
- [CortePaz](https://acumen.org/patient-capital/) — applies long-term enterprise support to sustainable cacao production and improved farming practices in Colombia. [^d4qy5h]
- [Ed Partners](https://acumen.org/patient-capital/) — provides financing connected to education businesses and expanded access to schooling in East Africa. [^d4qy5h]
- [Ziqitza Healthcare](https://acumen.org/patient-capital/) — illustrates patient capital in healthcare infrastructure and emergency medical services in India. [^d4qy5h]

## Case Studies

**Acumen and poverty-focused enterprises.** Acumen institutionalized patient capital as an impact-investing practice by combining philanthropic funding with investments in enterprises serving low-income communities. [^22bic9] [^d4qy5h] Its portfolio includes Haqdarshak, which trained 39,000 agents and helped unlock \$2.1 billion in benefits in India; CortePaz, whose customers report improved farming practices; Ed Partners, whose client schools serve 120,000 students; and Ziqitza Healthcare, which reports reaching 48 million people. [^d4qy5h] The cases show that patient capital is not simply “slow venture capital”: it accommodates business models whose success depends on trust, distribution networks, institutional partnerships, and gradual ecosystem formation. [^d62169] [^22bic9]

**The Engine and transformative technology.** Conventional venture capital often pressures startups toward rapid results and early exits, a pattern that can fit software but is less suitable for regulated or capital-intensive fields. [^l0svvm] The Engine was created to provide patient capital to ventures working on physical technologies, climate, health, and advanced systems that may require many years or decades before profitability. [^l0svvm] The case demonstrates how changing the financing timetable can expand the range of innovations that reach commercialization rather than forcing technically promising companies into premature exits or abandonment. [^l0svvm]

**Nesta and Featurespace.** Nesta began investment operations in 2007 after initially focusing on grants to individual innovators, creating a foundation-linked model that could invest in early-stage technology companies. [^22bic9] Its investment in Featurespace began in 2010, and Visa acquired the UK artificial-intelligence company in December 2024 after roughly 14 years of development and ownership. [^22bic9] The case illustrates patient capital’s central trade-off: accepting a long holding period can preserve the opportunity for a technology company to mature and scale before a major liquidity event. [^22bic9]
